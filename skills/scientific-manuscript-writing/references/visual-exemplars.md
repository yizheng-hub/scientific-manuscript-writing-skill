# Synthetic visual exemplars

Use the bundled figures as composition references, not as chart templates to copy mechanically. They contain invented geometry and values, no study-specific data, and no inference to report in a manuscript.

## Map-led evidence chain

Open `../assets/visual-exemplars/map-led-evidence.svg` before designing a spatial figure.

Transferable features:

- one dominant analytical map establishes the spatial claim;
- boundaries are context while layered fills, symbols, flows, and an inset carry evidence;
- supporting distribution, interval, and joint-relationship panels explain heterogeneity and uncertainty;
- panel areas reflect scientific importance rather than an automatic equal grid;
- one restrained semantic palette is reused across different chart families;
- exact `(a) Title` headers, Times New Roman, compact gaps, and integrated frameless legends.

## Mixed evidence grammar

Open `../assets/visual-exemplars/mixed-evidence.svg` before designing a non-spatial multi-panel figure.

Transferable features:

- a response surface is used because the joint relation matters, not as a decorative heatmap;
- a raincloud-like distribution, effect-size intervals, and a compact flow/decomposition panel reveal different aspects of one result;
- the layout uses one wide anchor and smaller explanatory panels instead of repeating a 2-by-2 grid;
- visual density comes from meaningful layers, not tiny text or ornamental marks;
- color, shape, position, and uncertainty are coordinated and remain readable in grayscale.

## Required comparison before accepting a figure

Ask four questions:

1. What is the visual anchor, and can a reader identify the main finding within five seconds?
2. Do supporting panels explain a different scientific relation, or are they cosmetic variants of the same chart?
3. If spatial data exist, does the map carry real evidence at a useful scale rather than acting as a pale locator icon?
4. Does the exported figure match the exemplars' hierarchy, density, typography, and compactness while remaining faithful to the current data?

Fail the figure when the answers expose repeated default charts, an equal grid with no hierarchy, excessive unused canvas, a tiny map, undefined visual encodings, bare panel letters, non-Times New Roman text, or any overlap.
